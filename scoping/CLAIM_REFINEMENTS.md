# Claim Refinements: What Would Make Each Claim Stronger

Constructive review of `paper/paper.tex` against the experiments in `scoping/`. All numbers assumed correct as computed. Items are ranked highest impact-per-cost first. Costs are agent execution time plus compute; everything runs locally on existing data unless flagged.

## Claim 1: Log-normal fits best on public data (Section 5)

- **[STRENGTHEN]** *Full-population run.* `fit.py` and `fit_rnd.py` sample 300 VMs per trace (265/268 retained) out of 1,250 + ~500; drop the `random.sample` and fit every VM, turning "68.35% of 12,479 bins on a sample" into the full census and removing the only sampling caveat a reviewer can raise. Cost: 30-60 min local CPU, one script edit per trace. Gain: eliminates "why a subsample of a public trace?" entirely.
- **[STRENGTHEN]** *Cluster bootstrap CI on the winner share.* Bins within a VM are correlated; resample VMs with replacement from `bitbrains_fit_results.csv` / `rnd_fit_results.csv` and report a 95% CI on the 68.35% share (and on the per-trace shares). Cost: 10-15 min, analysis of existing CSVs only. Gain: converts a point estimate into an interval a statistics-minded reviewer cannot ask for again.
- **[SHARPEN]** *Scope the claim to the family set.* State "best among four two-parameter families {normal, gamma, Weibull, log-normal}" everywhere the share is quoted (abstract already does; Section 5 prose and captions should too), and note that with all families at k = 2 the AIC ranking is exactly the likelihood ranking. Cost: 10 min prose. Gain: pre-empts "did you try log-logistic / inverse Gaussian?" by making the comparison set explicit.
- **[CONTROL]** *Sensitivity to preprocessing constants.* Rerun the fit at CPU_FLOOR in {0.01, 0.05, 0.1} and MIN_N in {60, 120}, reporting the winner share for each cell in one small appendix table; the floor interacts with the log transform and is the obvious knob a skeptic reaches for. Cost: 45-90 min local CPU. Gain: shows the headline share is not an artifact of the floor that makes log defined.
- **[PRESENT]** *Fix the stale Discussion paragraph and add a QQ exemplar.* The "From KS to modern goodness-of-fit" paragraph says applying A-D/CvM "would let us" report tail acceptance, but Table 3 (`ad_cvm.py`) already applies them; rewrite as a statement of what was done. Add one QQ-plot figure (log-normal vs. Gaussian on a representative busy bin) so the right-skew is visible, not just tabulated. Cost: 20-30 min. Gain: removes an internal inconsistency a careful reviewer will flag, and gives the fit claim a picture.

## Claim 2: Parameter scaling is directionally consistent with the mechanism (Section 7)

- **[STRENGTHEN]** *CIs on the Spearman and on the 9x ratio.* Bootstrap VMs in `refit_ab_fastStorage.csv` / `refit_ab_rnd.csv` to put intervals on Spearman 0.82/0.94 and on the median alpha-ratio 8.9/9.4 (report IQR too). n = 51/47 is small enough that a reviewer will want the interval. Cost: 15 min on existing CSVs. Gain: the two headline numbers of the mechanism claim become interval-backed.
- **[CONTROL]** *Units/confounder check on the 9x ratio.* Both alpha-hats carry units of 1/lambda, so per-VM load scale could inflate the rank correlation and shift the ratio; recompute the Spearman on load-normalized coefficients (multiply each alpha by that VM's lambda range) and the partial Spearman controlling for median lambda. If it survives, say so in one sentence. Cost: 20-30 min. Gain: closes the most natural "is this just both coefficients scaling with load units?" objection.
- **[STRENGTHEN]** *Fit the demand-fluctuation form explicitly (Form C).* The paper attributes the excess to load-proportional demand fluctuation; `refit_form_ab.py` fits Forms A and B but never the implied extension sigma = sqrt(alpha^2 lambda + c^2 lambda^2 + sigma_eps^2). Fitting Form C per VM and showing it lifts median R^2 above the 0.10-0.15 of A/B would turn the attribution from narrative into a fitted model. Cost: 30-45 min. Gain: the mechanism story acquires its own goodness-of-fit number.
- **[SHARPEN]** *Binomial statement on 105/106.* Report the exact binomial CI (or p-value against a 50/50 null) for sigma increasing with lambda on 105 of 106 VMs, and name the one exception VM with one diagnostic sentence, matching the paper's inspect-the-failing-point convention. Cost: 10 min. Gain: quantifies near-unanimity and shows the outlier was examined.
- **[PRESENT]** *Promote the CLT simulation to a figure.* `clt_sim.py` / `clt_sim.png` verify Equation (4) in a controlled setting but appear only as a parenthetical; a small figure (simulated sigma(lambda) recovering the form, with the Bitbrains regime marked as the flat region) makes the "the pure Poisson term is small at 5-minute sampling" argument visual. Cost: 20 min. Gain: the derivation correction is seen to be verified, not asserted. The Borg test (`BORG_PLAN.md`) remains the decisive experiment but costs BigQuery dollars and days; flag it as future work, do not gate the paper on it.

## Claim 3: The log-normal ceiling beats standard sizing baselines (Section 7)

- **[CONTROL]** *One unified evaluation panel for all six methods.* `sizing_eval.py` uses 300 VMs with MIN_TRAIN_BIN 60 / MIN_TEST_BIN 12; `ml_baseline.py` uses 200 VMs with per-day features and its own bin filters (>= 100 train pairs, >= 6 test samples). The six rows of Table 4 therefore come from two different panels. Merge into one pass on one panel (same VM sample, same bin retention, one output CSV per trace, all six ceilings per bin) so every number in the table is co-computed, per the project's own comparison convention. Cost: 60-90 min including rerun. Gain: makes the table immune to the "are these rows comparable?" question; also enables paired tests.
- **[STRENGTHEN]** *Paired per-bin significance test.* On the unified panel, run a sign test (or McNemar on hit/miss of the 99.8% target) for lognorm vs. each baseline per bin, clustered by VM, plus a bootstrap CI on each share-at-target. Cost: 20 min once the panel exists. Gain: "83.0 vs. 76.1" becomes "83.0 vs. 76.1, p < ...", which is what an empirical-systems reviewer expects.
- **[CONTROL]** *Cost-matched coverage comparison (k-sweep frontier).* Lognorm's own median relative cost is 58.2% on fastStorage (`\SzFsLNMedRelCost`), the highest in the panel, while the text emphasizes rolling_max's 19.5%; a reviewer who computes the missing column will push on this. Sweep k (and the quantile levels of the baselines) to trace each method's coverage-vs-ceiling frontier and show lognorm dominates at matched cost, or state plainly that it buys coverage with headroom. Cost: 45-60 min. Gain: converts the paper's most exposed flank into its strongest exhibit.
- **[CONTROL]** *Strengthen the GBM baseline before declaring it dominated.* The GBM uses 40 trees, depth 3, four aggregate features; add a light hyperparameter sweep and a per-day-max target variant. If lognorm still wins at target share, the win is over a tuned baseline; report the tuned number. Cost: 30-45 min. Gain: removes "the ML baseline was weak by construction".
- **[PRESENT]** *Add the relative-cost column to Table 4.* All six `MedRelCost` macros exist; showing them makes the coverage/cost trade-off explicit and honest in one table instead of scattered in prose. Cost: 10 min. Gain: reviewers see the full picture without recomputing it.

## Claim 4: Log-normal wins the SLA revenue simulation (Section 8)

- **[CONTROL]** *Extend the SLA table to all six methods.* `sla_sim.py` hard-codes `METHODS` to the four from `sizing_eval.py`; once the unified panel exists, GBM and EWMQ drop in for free, and the revenue claim covers the data-driven competitors too. Cost: 15 min after the unified panel. Gain: the economic conclusion covers the same field as the accuracy conclusion.
- **[STRENGTHEN]** *Bootstrap CI on net revenue and per-VM win rate.* Credits are a step function of availability, so means can be swung by a few VMs crossing a tier; resample VMs from `sla_fastStorage.csv` / `sla_rnd.csv` for a CI on each net-revenue ratio, and report the fraction of VMs where lognorm's credit is <= each baseline's. Cost: 15-20 min. Gain: shows the 87.05 vs. 81.12 gap is a population effect, not three unlucky VMs.
- **[CONTROL]** *Sensitivity to the coverage-equals-availability mapping.* The sim treats any test sample above the ceiling as failed service; add one alternative mapping (partial failure: only the demand fraction above the ceiling fails, using the Section 8 P_s(C) framing) and show the method ranking is unchanged. Cost: 45-60 min. Gain: defends the strongest modelling assumption in the pipeline.
- **[SHARPEN]** *Reconcile the window with monthly-uptime schedules.* The schedules are monthly; the evaluation window is six days. Either evaluate availability over rolling 6-day splits across the month (multiple split points, report spread) or state the window-to-month extrapolation as an explicit assumption. Cost: 30-60 min for rolling splits. Gain: closes the "you applied a monthly SLA to six days" objection.

## Claim 5: Practical stakes (Section 1 dollar paragraph)

- **[CONTROL]** *Make the "closes both gaps" sentence consistent with Table 4.* The paragraph implies the log-normal ceiling closes the over-provisioning gap, but its own median rel-cost (58.2% fastStorage) exceeds rolling_max's 19.5%. Either rewrite the second force around coverage-per-dollar using the cost-matched frontier from Claim 3, or scope the sentence to the coverage gap only. Cost: 20 min prose (plus the frontier). Gain: removes the one intro claim a reviewer can falsify from the paper's own macros.
- **[PRESENT]** *A worked dollar example.* One three-line table: a 1,000-VM fleet at a named on-demand price (e.g. m5.xlarge), annual billed revenue, credits under AWS at 87.05% vs. 81.12% net, dollars kept. The pricing citation is already promised in the abstract. Cost: 20-30 min (one price lookup; public data). Gain: the "seven-figure delta" becomes arithmetic the reviewer can check.
- **[STRENGTHEN]** *External prevalence citation.* One sentence citing a public utilization/over-provisioning survey (e.g. a published cloud utilization report or the Resource Central utilization figures already cited) to establish that the headroom problem is general, not trace-specific. Cost: 15-20 min with `web-researcher` + `bibtest`. Gain: the stakes rest on more than this paper's two traces.
- **[SHARPEN]** *Pin the 5.9pp arithmetic to the table.* State that 5.9pp = 87.05 - 81.12 (fastStorage, AWS, Table 5) with a cross-reference, so the intro number is visibly derived rather than free-floating. Cost: 5 min. Gain: every intro number becomes traceable to a table.

## Cross-cutting refinements (best value per effort)

1. **Unified one-pass evaluation artifact.** One script, one VM panel, one bin filter, all six sizing methods, emitting a single per-bin CSV that feeds Table 4, the SLA simulation, and the intro dollar numbers. Serves Claims 3, 4, and 5 at once and implements the project's own construct-matched comparison rule. ~90 min total.
2. **Cluster-bootstrap CIs everywhere.** VM-level resampling on the four existing result CSVs puts intervals on the 68.35% share, the Spearmans and the 9x ratio, share-at-target, and net revenue. Serves Claims 1-4 for ~45 min combined, pure analysis of files already on disk.
3. **Full-population fits.** Removing the 300-VM subsampling in `fit.py`, `fit_rnd.py`, and (after unification) the sizing scripts upgrades every "on a sample of" number to a census of both public traces. ~1-2 hours local CPU, mostly unattended.
4. **Coverage-cost frontier figure.** A k-sweep per method, plotted as coverage vs. median ceiling, lands Claims 3 and 5 simultaneously and neutralizes the rel-cost exposure.

## If we could only do one thing

Build the unified one-pass evaluation panel and rerun sizing plus SLA on it with cluster-bootstrap CIs. It is the single change that touches the most claims: it makes the six-method table internally comparable, extends the revenue simulation to the data-driven baselines, supplies the significance statements for both the 83%-vs-76% and the 87%-vs-81% headlines, and produces the per-bin data needed for the cost-matched frontier that fixes the intro's over-provisioning sentence. Roughly 2-3 hours of agent execution, all local CPU, no new data or dependencies.
